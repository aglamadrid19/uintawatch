We didn't kill the project. We changed what it claims to be.

## The reframe

The stress test's strongest conclusion was that we had aimed at the *hardest* version of the problem — an autonomous statewide wildfire alarm network — while the evidence pointed at a different, real, and winnable niche. We're re-aiming.

> **UintaWatch is an open, off-grid sensing lab for the detection gaps that cameras and satellites provably cannot cover: night smoldering, cloud-obscured events, low-intensity smoldering on contained or "extinguished" fires, and far-field smoke discrimination.**

## What published research says we can aim at

A January 2026 study in *Fire* (Natural Resources Canada, 9(4):141) field-tested continuous ground smoke sensors across 20 prescribed fires and wildfires — and the sensors were purchased by **DHS S&T**, the same program the IG audit condemned. The results give us **external, published bars to clear** instead of self-referential success:

| Published result | Our target on open hardware |
|---|---|
| 100% detection of fires >100 ha at median ~1.5 km | Match it with ~$50–100 open nodes and LoRa instead of internet backhaul |
| 30% detection of fires ≤1 ha | Improve via multi-node corroboration and wind-aware placement |
| Nighttime smoldering detected where satellites see nothing | Primary target — this is where we aim |
| Far-field regional smoke = major unsolved false-positive source | Solve it with open data and published algorithms |
| Closed vendor hardware, internet backhaul | Open hardware, off-grid — the unclaimed difference |

*Sensors purchased by DHS S&T, deployed across 20 fires in Canada, 2023–2024 (Fire 9(4):141).*

Clearing those bars is a positive, publishable result. If some turn out unreachable, that's a published boundary — useful to everyone, still a win for the lab.

## What changes in public

- Our website copy now labels hypotheses as hypotheses. The BME688 is **one input under test**, never "the fire detector."
- We added an [Open Questions](/open-questions) page: what published research already shows, and what we're measuring next.
- We're starting from data, not hardware: the Canadian study's full dataset is open (MIT license), so the lab's first experiments can happen before a single node is built.
- Meshtastic stays as one tested transport — not a sacred architecture.

## What doesn't change

Open source, build-in-public, and the conviction that Utah's detection gaps deserve better instrumentation. The next entry lays out the working roadmap.
